## Orientation is cumulative

Many people imagine orientation as a single day when they are asked to choose a stream. In reality, the decision starts with noticing which subjects you understand and enjoy, reviewing your results, considering your circumstances, and gathering information about pathways and opportunities.

The earlier you start, the less likely you are to choose under pressure or based on unverified claims.

## Key stages

| Stage | Main question | What to verify |
|---|---|---|
| End of primary school | Transition to middle school | Receiving school and administrative steps |
| End of middle school | Which secondary pathway fits? | Conditions and pathways available at your school |
| Common core year | Which science, literature or technical track? | Subjects and progression requirements |
| End of baccalaureate | Which higher education or training? | Thresholds, pathways and vocational options |
| After training | Work or further study | Qualifications, opportunities and recognition |

These are general stages. Names and procedures may vary by region, school and ongoing education reforms.

## Gathering information

Use several sources, ordered by reliability:

1. **The school:** direct information on conditions and available pathways.
2. **The Ministry and regional academies:** official announcements and procedures.
3. **Official platforms:** resources and notices.
4. **Visits and fairs:** useful for understanding training, but verify recognition.
5. **Personal experience:** helpful context, not a universal rule.

Be careful with:

- "Guaranteed future" promises in exchange for money.
- Old information about pathways that no longer exist.
- Comparing your results with others without context.
- Decisions based only on family pressure.

## Questions for the school

- Which pathways are actually available at this school?
- What are the conditions for entry or progression?
- What are the core subjects and teaching hours?
- What study options follow this pathway?
- Is there a nearby vocational training alternative?
- What are the deadlines and required documents?
- Who can help me understand my results?

Write down the answers rather than relying on memory. If the answer is "we will see later", ask for a meeting or a written reference.

## The family's role

The family is an important partner, but the decision should respect the student's abilities and interests. It helps to:

- Discuss results calmly.
- Visit institutions where possible.
- Ask school specialists.
- Avoid humiliating comparisons.
- Avoid imposing a pathway only because of social pressure.

## No absolute guarantees

No guide can guarantee success or employment. Orientation involves probabilities and is affected by results, skills, economic conditions and public policy. The goal is a considered decision, not a promise.

> [!NOTE] Information changes
> Pathways and conditions may change from one year to the next. Check the official announcements for the current school year, and do not rely on an old article.

## Summary

Start early, gather information from the school and official sources, ask specific questions, and balance interests, results and opportunities. Do not buy promises, and do not decide under the pressure of the moment.
