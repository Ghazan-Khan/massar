## Why a separate section?

Education news is sensitive because it shapes decisions made by families and students. One inaccurate report about an exam date or a result can cause widespread confusion. That is why we focus on **explanation and verification** rather than competing to publish first.

## What you will find here

- Guides on how to verify education news before sharing it.
- Advice on preparing for the school year.
- Explanations of the difference between an official announcement, a leak and a prediction.
- Links to official sources you can consult yourself.

## What you will not find here

- Unofficial result announcements.
- Holiday or exam dates we have not verified.
- Screenshots reproduced without a source.
- Promises or paid services.

> [!NOTE] The official source comes first
> If a story concerns an official procedure, the reference is the official announcement from the ministry or the institution. Our articles help you understand it, but they do not replace it.

## How we choose topics

We write when a question keeps coming up and a clear explanation can prevent a mistake: how to check a viral claim, how to prepare for the start of the year, how to tell a proposal from a decision. We do not chase traffic with alarming headlines, and we do not invent quotes or sources.

## Reading an education story in 2026

Most confusion comes from three things: an old article resurfacing, a screenshot without context, and a real announcement stretched beyond what it says. Our verification guide walks through each check. The preparation guide covers what families can control, such as registration, accounts and routine, instead of waiting for dates that may change.

Start with the verification guide if a claim is circulating in a group chat. Start with the preparation guide if you are organising the beginning of the year.
