## Before you search

Baccalaureate results are a common subject for unverified news. Before searching, distinguish between:

- **An official announcement:** issued by the Ministry, an academy or a school, with a link and a date.
- **A leak or copied screenshot:** possibly old or edited, and not suitable for verification.
- **A comment or prediction:** not a result, even if written in an official style.

Do not rely on an image with no known source, or on a link sent by someone you do not know.

## Where results are announced

The competent authorities usually announce results through official channels. These may include the Ministry website, a dedicated exam portal, or a notice from the school. The channel can change from one session to another.

Therefore:

1. Start from the Ministry website or the official page it announces.
2. Check that the link refers to the correct session and year.
3. Do not enter your details on a page reached through an advertisement.
4. Follow the school's instructions for collecting documents.

## Normal and resit sessions

The normal session is the main attempt. The resit session is organised later for specific categories, under conditions and results set by the competent authority. Do not assume that every candidate is eligible, and do not rely on dates circulating without an official announcement.

If you are waiting for the resit session, check:

- The official announcement of eligibility.
- Instructions from your school.
- Required documents and deadlines.
- Any conditions related to registration or attendance.

## After the result appears

- Keep a copy of the official document if possible.
- Check your personal details and stream.
- Read any school notes about registration or orientation.
- If you are eligible for the resit session, follow the instructions carefully.
- Do not publish the result with full personal details.

## If the result is unexpected

You may feel disappointed or angry, but a rushed reaction can harm you. Start by:

1. Confirming that the result belongs to you and the correct session.
2. Reading the official notice about calculation and review.
3. Contacting the school to understand your options.
4. Respecting appeal or review deadlines if available.
5. Exploring orientation or training alternatives without relying on unofficial promises.

> [!NOTE] No guaranteed promises
> No website can guarantee that you will pass or change your result. Be wary of anyone asking for money to "adjust" or "rescue" a result.

## Common scams

- Messages asking for your password to show a result.
- Websites requesting identity documents or birth certificates.
- Accounts selling "guaranteed results".
- Short links that imitate the Ministry website.
- Payment requests for a "fast review".

The rule is simple: an official result does not need a middleman and cannot be bought.

## Summary

Check baccalaureate results through official channels only, distinguish the normal and resit sessions, keep the documents, and respect deadlines. Do not share your details or pay for promises. This website explains; it does not announce results.
