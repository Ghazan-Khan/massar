## Start with the official source

The school and the teacher are the first reference for lessons and deadlines. Official platforms such as the Ministry website and Telmidtice may provide additional resources or notices, but they do not replace attendance and discussion in class.

Do not rely on unknown links or "guaranteed summaries" sold for money. A good resource should be:

- Connected to the curriculum or an official announcement.
- Clear about its source and date.
- Free of unnecessary requests for personal data.
- Appropriate for your level.

## Managing time

Try a simple weekly plan rather than an ideal timetable you cannot follow:

| Day | Main task | Short review |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | New lesson and exercises | 20 minutes |
| Tuesday | Review Monday's lesson | 20 minutes |
| Wednesday | Applied exercises | 20 minutes |
| Thursday | Cumulative review | 20 minutes |
| Friday | Light reading or summary | 15 minutes |
| Saturday | Weekly review | 45 minutes |
| Sunday | Rest and planning | 15 minutes |

This is an example, not a rule. Adjust it to your schedule and circumstances. Consistency matters more than a single long session.

## Effective revision

- **Active recall:** close the book and explain the lesson aloud.
- **Spaced practice:** review after a day, then a week, then a month.
- **Questions:** write questions instead of rereading passively.
- **Varied exercises:** solve different types of problems, not only one pattern.
- **Teach someone:** explaining a concept reveals your gaps.
- **Breaks:** study for 25-40 minutes, then take a short break.

## Preparing for exams

1. Start early enough.
2. Collect official information about the date, place and permitted materials.
3. Review past mistakes instead of repeating what you already know.
4. Sleep well, and do not stay up all night before the exam.
5. Read the instructions before answering.
6. Keep time for a final review.

Do not look for "leaks" or "guaranteed questions". They may be misleading or illegal, and they can expose you to penalties.

## Digital skills

- Use strong, unique passwords.
- Do not share account details with friends.
- Check links before entering information.
- Do not install apps from untrusted sources.
- Respect other people's rights when sharing content.
- Take breaks from screens.

## Health and focus

Enough sleep, movement and balanced food affect learning. If you feel persistent stress or anxiety that affects your studies, talk to someone you trust or a professional. Asking for help is not weakness.

## Summary

The best resource is one you can use consistently and that deepens your understanding. Start with the school and official sources, organise your time, test yourself and protect your data. Do not buy promises or rely on unknown links.
